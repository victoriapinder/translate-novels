# German (Deutschland/Österreich/Schweiz) — Liebesroman

- Address: strangers, business, older people, the hero's boss/coach/agent → Sie. The romantic couple starts on Sie
  ONLY if the source has them formal (Mr./Ms., strangers, professional distance); they switch to du at the first
  clearly intimate or friendly beat (first name offered, first kiss, first real personal talk) and NEVER revert.
  Siblings, close friends, family, teammates, children → du from the first line. Siblings → du always.
- Honorifics: Mr. → Herr, Mrs./Ms. → Frau, Dr. → Dr. (Herr Dr./Frau Dr. only in formal address). "Miss" → Fräulein is
  banned; use Frau or the first name.
- Quotation marks: „…“ for dialogue; ‚…‘ for quotes inside quotes. Dialogue tags: „…“, sagte er. Em-dash → Gedankenstrich
  with spaces (Wort – Wort). Ellipsis „…“ with a space before it.
- Genre vocabulary: German words only, on covers, titles, blurbs and metadata (Milliardär, geheimes Baby, Scheinehe, zweite
  Chance, Rockstar, Sportler) — never the English trope words, even though German bestsellers use them (house rule).
- Pet names: "babe/baby" → "Baby" (used in German romance) or "Schatz" if the tone is tender; "sweetheart" → "Süße/Süßer"
  sparingly, "Schatz" preferred; "honey" → "Schatz". Never "Liebling" for a new couple. "cara"/foreign pet names stay.
- Register: modern, natural spoken German; no "Anglizismen" where a German word is normal (not "Date" when "Verabredung"
  reads better in the sentence, but "Date" is acceptable in casual dialogue); avoid the passive; avoid "man" constructions
  where the source is "I".
- Sports: baseball/football/hockey terms stay in English (Pitcher, Homerun, Quarterback, Touchdown, Playoffs); "the team"
  → "das Team"/"die Mannschaft".
- Measures → metric (Meter, Kilometer, Kilo, Grad Celsius). "a foot away" → "einen Schritt entfernt".
- Numbers under 13 in words; German date/time forms (halb acht, 19 Uhr).
- Titles/chapter headings: "Chapter 3" → "Kapitel 3"; "Epilogue" → "Epilog"; "Prologue" → "Prolog".
- Never: "Ms.", "okay" spelled "ok" (write "okay"), literal "einen Fuß entfernt", literal idioms, comma splices.
